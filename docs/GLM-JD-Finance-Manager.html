Finance Manager

Keeps the money and the books: collections, payments, expenses, payroll, statutory returns and the monthly close.

1Purpose of the role

The Finance Manager is responsible for the accuracy of the books and for the business's money: that every sale and payment is recorded, customers pay on time, suppliers and staff are paid correctly, statutory payments and returns are made by their due dates, and the books are closed and reported each month. The system posts the books from the day's work; your job is to make sure the inputs are right, the controls are followed, and the monthly figures can be relied on.

2Key responsibilities

Collections and credit control

  • Prepare quotations and invoices for corporate clients (Finance → Quotation / Invoice) and keep the Corporate Clients register — credit days, contacts — up to date.
  • Work the Receivables ageing: chase overdue invoices, agree payment plans, and escalate to the General Manager.
  • Issue credit notes and debit notes (itemised, restocked where goods return) with the right approval; never alter a recorded sale.
  • Reconcile payments: M-Pesa matching, NCBA notifications, bank and card receipts against the orders; clear exceptions daily.

Expenses, suppliers and petty cash

  • Capture expenses under the right head, with the business head, VAT treatment and withholding tax; record supplier bills on credit with the supplier, invoice number and due date, and pay them by the due date (Owed / Due).
  • Manage the petty-cash float: top-ups, and that the float covers wages and purchases on the dates they fall.
  • Raise amendments and deletion requests with a reason for wrong entries; they are decided by a different manager. Keep the supporting invoices and receipts.
  • Review the Payables ageing and supplier terms with the General Manager.

Purchases and stock accounting

  • Approve or reject purchase requisitions (never your own); check each purchase against the supplier's invoice and the quantity received.
  • Review Purchases Reconciliation variances (price and quantity) and stock-take differences; investigate and report.
  • Cost imports with the landed-cost calculator before prices are set.

Payroll and staff pay

  • Keep each employee's National ID, KRA PIN, SHIF number and gross salary on record (Compliance → Employees).
  • Enter the monthly payroll for fixed-salary employees and casuals, print the register and payslips, and issue the P9 at year end.
  • Approve each month in Commission → Team & payouts so pay-band staff go into payroll — with the minimum wage applied — before payday (the 5th); pay commission and freelance payouts, with withholding tax deducted.

Statutory compliance

  • Prepare the VAT position (output less input) each month and pay by the 20th.
  • Use Compliance → Payments due to write the monthly cheque: PAYE, SHIF and the housing levy by the 9th, NSSF by the 15th, VAT and withholding tax by the 20th, and income-tax instalments; record each remittance as a journal.
  • Maintain the corporation-tax (or turnover-tax) schedule: enter the adjustments, estimate and capital allowances, and record each payment to KRA with its reference. Confirm treatment with the external accountant.

Accounting and month-end close

  • Keep the chart of accounts, journals (opening balances, adjustments, remittances), the asset register and depreciation correct.
  • At month-end: all orders paid or invoiced, all expenses recorded, payroll entered, stock counted, M-Pesa matched, then run Books Check until clean.
  • Produce the monthly pack for the General Manager: Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Receivables, Payables, VAT position, payroll and commission cost, and margin by line of business.

Control

  • Apply segregation of duties: you do not approve your own requests or receive purchases you captured.
  • Report anomalies and any shortfall or difference at once; keep the audit trail clean.

3Authority and limits

What you can do in the system is set by your role and is checked on the server at every request.

You can

  • Open Finance, Compliance, Accounting and Reports
  • See every order and all supplier costs
  • Capture expenses, payroll, quotations and invoices
  • Issue credit and debit notes; post journals
  • Approve stock requisitions; receive purchases
  • Manage DTF rolls and price approvals
  • Approve commission months and pay them
  • Manage petty cash, assets and corporate clients

You need a second person for

  • Amending or deleting an expense, payroll entry or petty-cash top-up you raised
  • Receiving a purchase you captured
  • A requisition or price approval you raised yourself
  • Anything involving your own pay

You cannot

  • Open Master Data: staff, roles, price lists, integrations, backups, security
  • Change commission bands, target, minimum wage or payday (Admin only)
  • Approve your own requests
  • Delete or change a recorded sale

5Remuneration

  • Your pay is set out in your contract of employment and run through payroll each month, with PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and the housing levy deducted. As you handle payroll, your own pay should be processed and approved by someone else.
  • Any bonus, allowance or incentive arrangement is as agreed in writing with the General Manager or owner.

6Standards and conduct

  • Your PIN is yours. Never share it, never sign in as someone else, and sign out when you leave the screen.
  • Every record is traceable. The system records who captured, changed or approved each order, payment, expense and claim; accuracy and honesty in what you enter are part of the job.
  • Accuracy and timeliness. Statutory dates and the 5th-of-the-month payday are not negotiable; plan the close around them.
  • Segregation of duties. Never approve your own entries, receive your own purchases or process your own pay.
  • Keep the paper trail. Every expense, bill and remittance has its supporting document and its reference in the system.
  • Customer and staff information is confidential and used only for the work it was given for.
  • Raise problems early — a wrong figure, a disputed payment, a control that is not being followed — with your manager.

8Typical requirements

  • A professional accounting qualification (for example CPA(K) or ACCA) or a degree in accounting or finance.
  • Experience keeping books and closing months, with reconciliations, receivables and payables.
  • Good working knowledge of Kenyan tax and statutory deductions: VAT, PAYE, NSSF, SHIF, the housing levy, withholding tax and corporation or turnover tax.
  • Proficient with accounting software and spreadsheets; careful, deadline-driven and discreet with confidential information.
  • Integrity and sound judgement; able to explain figures to non-finance colleagues.
