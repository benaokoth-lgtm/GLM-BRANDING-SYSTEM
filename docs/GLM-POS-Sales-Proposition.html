The problemBranding shops make good money and lose a lot of it

Most shops run on a mix of notebooks, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet and a generic till. It works until the shop is busy — and that is exactly when money leaks.

"Who gave that discount?" Prices and discounts are set at the counter, with no limit and no trail.
"Which line actually makes money?" DTF, UV, embroidery and banners all share one till, so the owner guesses where the profit is.
"How much film did we waste this month?" Rolls are bought, used and finished without anyone measuring cost per roll, waste or margin.
"Did that customer pay?" M-Pesa messages on someone's phone, deposits in a notebook, corporate invoices chased from memory.
"What do we owe KRA this month?" VAT, PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and the housing levy are worked out in a rush, and the cheque is a guess.
"Why is the stock out again?" Reorders happen when the shelf is empty; purchases are not checked against what arrived.
"Is my sales team motivated?" Commission is argued over, because nobody can show exactly what each person sold.

The answerOne system. One record. Every step connected.

An order entered once at the counter is also the sale in the books, the VAT on the return, the job on the production floor, the stock deducted, and the commission earned.

Why it pays for itselfEight reasons owners switch

1 · Protects your price

Minimum prices on film, artwork and embroidery are enforced on the server. A discount above your ceiling is flagged. Lowering a price needs a named manager's approval, and nobody approves their own.

2 · Shows true profit by line

Real cost of sales (purchases and supplier bills), not a guessed percentage, by business head. Know whether embroidery, DTF or banners deserve the next machine.

3 · Collects cash faster

M-Pesa STK push at the counter (Safaricom Daraja or NCBA Paybill), split payments, deposits, Pending Payments, ageing receivables, and automatic M-Pesa statement matching.

4 · Knows Kenya's taxes

VAT automatic from sales and purchases; PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and housing levy payroll with payslips and P9; a monthly Payments due total to write one cheque; corporation tax or turnover tax schedules; withholding tax on freelancers.

5 · Real accounting, not a bolt-on

Double-entry books that post themselves: profit & loss, balance sheet, cash flow, trial balance, ledger, journals, credit and debit notes, depreciation, and a books check — ready for your accountant.

6 · Controls production

Jobs are assigned in-house or sent to suppliers, inspected before release, reworked when they fail, and only handed over after they pass. No order is "completed" by hand.

7 · Motivates the sales team

Commission on money received, a clear pay-band ladder, a performance floor with warnings, weekly pay for freelance sales persons with tax withheld, and automatic payroll with a minimum-wage floor.

8 · Keeps people honest

Role-based access, maker-and-checker approvals on expenses, payroll and stock, an audit trail, scheduled backups with one-click restore, and an emailed sign-in code for Admins.

Day to dayWhat a busy Saturday looks like

  • 8:30 — The cashier takes three walk-in orders. The receipt prints on the 80 mm thermal printer (two copies) as the M-Pesa prompt confirms on the customer's phone.
  • 10:00 — A corporate client asks for 300 embroidered polos. The sales person enters the stitch count; the system shows the stitch price and the lower quantity price. Choosing the lower one sends it to the manager's approval queue; the quotation goes to the client by email as a PDF.
  • 11:30 — The supervisor assigns the waiting jobs. A banner job goes to your outside printer; the supplier's bill is recorded against it.
  • 14:00 — A DTF job fails inspection and goes back to production as rework. The failure is on record.
  • 16:00 — The owner opens Sales by Business Head and sees today's margin by line, without asking anyone for a report.
  • Month-end — Finance opens Payments due, writes one cheque for PAYE, NSSF, SHIF, levy, VAT and tax, and runs Books Check. The accountant gets statements that already balance.

Questions owners askStraight answers

"My team is not good with computers."

The counter screens are simple: pick the item, enter the quantity, take the payment. PIN-pad sign-in, one searchable item list, a calculator for artwork size, and prices that fill in for you. Each role sees only the screens it needs.

"We already have an accountant."

Good — they will like it. The books post themselves, statements always balance, the VAT return and payroll schedules agree with the ledger, and Books Check flags anything out of line. They keep doing the filing and the advice.

"We are small. Is this too much?"

Switch on only what you need. A single-counter shop can start with orders, payments and receipts, and add stock, payroll and accounting as it grows. Roles and menus adjust automatically.

"What if the internet is slow or we change premises?"

It runs in a browser against your server, and lists are light and paged. Backups can be downloaded at any time and restored to a new server.

"Can it match how we price?"

Prices come from your own price list: per piece, per sqm, per metre, per stitch. Rates, bands, minimum prices, the discount ceiling, the commission ladder and the minimum wage are all settings, not code.
