General Manager

Runs the business end to end — sales, production, stock, finance and people — and is accountable for profit, cash and compliance.

1Purpose of the role

The General Manager is accountable for the whole business: that it sells at the right prices, makes what it sells on time and to standard, buys well, collects its money, pays what it owes when it is due, and makes a profit. You set direction with the owner, lead the Finance Manager, Supervisor, Front Office and sales team, take the decisions that need seniority, and use the system's reports to see where the business stands and where to act.

2Key responsibilities

Profit and performance

  • Review the Profit & Loss (accrual and cash) and Reports → Sales by Business Head every month: sales, cost and margin for DTF, UV, embroidery, large format and laser. Decide where to push, reprice or stop.
  • Review DTF → Dashboard (roll profit, wastage, what discounts cost) and Reports → Embroidery Profitability.
  • Set targets with the owner and track them; keep the monthly sales floor and the sales team's results in front of the sales staff.

Sales and people

  • Review staff results in Commission → Team & payouts and the Monthly report; coach anyone marked needs improvement and agree a plan with them.
  • Approve each month's commission so pay-band staff go into payroll before payday (the 5th); recommend any change to the bands, target or minimum wage to the Admin, who alone sets them.
  • Lead, coach and appraise your direct reports; recommend hiring, role changes and training.

Operations: production, quality and delivery

  • Oversee the production queue and who is assigned what (Production), including outsourced jobs and suppliers; keep promised delivery dates.
  • Review Quality Control results: pass rate, failures and rework; act on recurring defects.
  • Manage supplier relationships and costs; review the quotes and mark-ups on contracted-out work.

Stock and purchasing

  • Act on reorder alerts; approve or reject purchase requisitions (never your own); review purchase reconciliation variances and stock-take differences.
  • Keep the import and purchasing decisions (e.g. China imports) inside what the landed cost and margin allow.

Money and compliance oversight

  • Review receivables ageing and payables, cash flow and the petty-cash position; decide on credit terms for corporate clients.
  • Make sure statutory payments are made on time: PAYE, SHIF and the housing levy by the 9th, NSSF by the 15th, VAT and withholding tax by the 20th, and income-tax instalments — the monthly Compliance → Payments due total is the cheque to write.
  • Review the VAT position, the corporation-tax or turnover-tax schedule and the payroll with the Finance Manager.

Approvals and control

  • Decide DTF artwork and embroidery price approvals and discount exceptions, never for work you captured yourself.
  • Approve (as the second person) expense, payroll and petty-cash amendments and deletions raised by others; never approve your own.
  • Keep the controls working: segregation of duties, supporting documents, and follow-up of exceptions. Ask the Admin for the audit log and for backups to be confirmed.

3Authority and limits

What you can do in the system is set by your role and is checked on the server at every request.

You can

  • See every order and every report
  • See supplier costs and mark-ups
  • Open Finance, Compliance, Accounting and Reports
  • Assign and manage production; open Quality Control
  • Approve stock requisitions
  • Manage DTF rolls, costs and price approvals
  • Approve commission months and payouts
  • Capture expenses, payroll, quotations and invoices

You need a second person for

  • Amending or deleting an expense, payroll entry or petty-cash top-up you raised
  • Receiving a purchase you captured
  • A price approval or requisition you raised yourself
  • Anything involving your own pay

You cannot

  • Open Master Data: staff, roles, price lists, discount rules, company details, integrations, backups, security
  • Change commission bands, target, minimum wage or payday (Admin only)
  • Approve your own requests
  • Delete a recorded sale

5Remuneration

  • Your pay is set out in your contract of employment and run through payroll each month, with PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and the housing levy deducted.
  • Any bonus, allowance or incentive arrangement is as agreed in writing with the owner.

6Standards and conduct

  • Your PIN is yours. Never share it, never sign in as someone else, and sign out when you leave the screen.
  • Every record is traceable. The system records who captured, changed or approved each order, payment, expense and claim; accuracy and honesty in what you enter are part of the job.
  • You set the example. Follow the same controls as everyone else: no approving your own requests, no off-system money, no exceptions to the pricing rules without a recorded approval.
  • Decisions leave a trace. Approvals, price exceptions and commission decisions are recorded in the system with your name; make them on the facts.
  • Customer and staff information is confidential and used only for the work it was given for.
  • Raise problems early — a wrong figure, a disputed payment, a control that is not being followed — with your manager.

8Typical requirements

  • A degree or diploma in business, commerce or a related field, or equivalent experience.
  • Experience managing a business or department, ideally in printing, manufacturing, branding or retail.
  • Able to read and act on a profit and loss, a cash flow and a receivables ageing.
  • Working knowledge of Kenyan statutory requirements: VAT, PAYE, NSSF, SHIF, the housing levy and income tax.
  • Leadership, sound judgement, integrity and good communication with staff, customers and suppliers.
